# Decisions

These are the decisions that shaped the system. Numbers come from the
working log I kept while building, and the other docs cite them. Related
decisions are merged into one row.

"Why" is in my words. "AI involvement" records what the assistant proposed
and what I did with it.

| #                 | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | AI involvement                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1                 | The domain is sportsbook odds.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | I already know how odds data varies between sources, and it's the data Pikkit itself works with.                                                                                                                                                                           | None.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 11, 14, 52, 53    | Two books, each reported by **both** its own direct feed and a third-party aggregator: three adapters with deliberately different schemas.                                                                                                                                                                                                    | The main problem to tackle is data inconsistencies, and two kinds of provider reporting the same books create them naturally. It also shows the direct feeds differing from each other, as well as from the aggregator.                                                    | **Reworked.** The AI framed each book as having one source. I redesigned it so the providers overlap.                                                                                                |
| 12, 67, 68        | Games match on the unordered pair of teams plus a start time within ±30 min. Team spellings live in `fixtures/*.csv`, validated at startup. A provider listing home and away reversed links to the same game with its sides flipped.                                                                                                          | Adding a provider should mean adding rows, not changing code.                                                                                                                                                                                                              | I chose alias-table matching. The data-file and home/away changes came from my question about how the design copes with new providers.                                                               |
| 15, 21, 28        | For each book, the direct feed wins while fresh, then the aggregator is used. A successful poll counts as a heartbeat. Each source has its own staleness threshold.                                                                                                                                                                           | We should trust our "internal" scripts more than "external" data providers. They are easier to validate and change.                                                                                                                                                        | The confidence rating was my idea. Precedence and heartbeats were accepted AI recommendations.                                                                                                       |
| 16, 17, 57        | The aggregator shows off the board by leaving a market out. A missing market counts as off the board **only after a complete fetch**. While it's still missing, the off-board record is only confirmed, not written again.                                                                                                                    | We don't want a broken fetch to mark odds OTB en-masse.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Accepted. The "confirm, don't re-infer" rule was the AI's fix for markets flipping back and forth under a literal reading of #25.                                                                    |
| 20, 54, 55        | Records that fail parsing or the margin check (1.00–1.15, when both sides are open) are quarantined with their raw JSON and a reason. A single open side is valid.                                                                                                                                                                            | A single side can legitimately go off the board.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | **Changed.** The AI's check required both sides; I pointed out one side can be off the board.                                                                                                        |
| 22, 25, 58        | When sources disagree, **err toward off the board**. A market's status comes from its most recent fresh observation; a newer open observation reopens it.                                                                                                                                                                                     | We'd rather wrongly show a market as off the board than show one as offered when it's actually off the board.                                                                                                                                                              | **Changed.** The AI's version kept a market off the board until the lagging aggregator caught up. I added the reopen rule.                                                                           |
| 31, 32            | "Newer" means the provider's timestamp, never the time we received it. A source with no timestamp uses the time we received it and is capped at medium confidence.                                                                                                                                                                            | In my time working with sportsbook data, rarely have I seen sequence numbers. Omitting them makes the system more realistic.                                                                                                                                               | **Rejected** the AI's assumption that feeds send sequence numbers.                                                                                                                             |
| 33, 35            | Every provider is polled as a **full refresh**, split into slices that match how each provider divides its data.                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Sportsbooks usually don't offer "changes since", and scraping them is normally a full poll. Splitting the polls limits the impact of faults and failures. Real providers split their data differently, and the slices still feed one shared ingestion pipeline.            | **Rejected.** The AI proposed polling direct feeds for "changes since" a timestamp.                                                                                                                  |
| 34                | Every successful poll moves `last_confirmed_at` forward, even when nothing changed. Unchanged data isn't stored again.                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | This reduces data storage required while keeping odds up to date. Ideally we could save a copy with an updated `last_confirmed_at` for line history.                                                                                                                       | My requirement.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 42                | Prices are stored as **full-precision decimal odds**, replacing an earlier choice of American integers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Full-precision decimal loses nothing for either kind of source.                                                                                                                                                                                                            | **Reworked.** The AI claimed 2-decimal odds convert back to the true American price. A test disproved it (−150 → 1.67 → −149, and 90% of favorites fail). It corrected itself and I chose to switch. |
| 36, 43–48         | A fixed worker pool per provider; per-source timeouts; exponential backoff with jitter per slice; a token-bucket budget per provider that pauses on 429 until `Retry-After`; the catalog refreshed periodically, keeping the last known slices if it fails; `retrying` and `failing` states on `/status`.                                     | Individual providers can be fickle, and shouldn't affect one another. Work should be isolated to the provider it's for.                                                                                                                                                    | **Changed.** The AI proposed one loop per slice; I chose a worker pool. Periodic catalog refresh was my addition.                                                                                    |
| 37–39, 49–51, 63  | The simulator: local HTTP mock servers in the same binary, seeded price drift, market suspensions, background problems on by default, four scripted scenarios, and an admin API on its own port.                                                                                                                                              |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Accepted AI recommendations.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| 56, 59            | A market that's off the board keeps its **last known price**; the `status` field is the main indicator.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Off the board is the main indicator; the last known price stays visible.                                                                                                                                                                                                   | **Rejected.** The AI proposed hiding the price.                                                                                                                                                      |
| 26, 27, 60–62, 70 | Confidence has four levels (`verified`, `high`, `medium`, `low`) with reasons from a fixed list. Sources agree when implied probabilities are within 1.5 points, and match when equal at the coarser source's precision (set per source). Comparisons use the direct feed's current price; allowing for aggregator lag was left out of scope. | It's worth ingesting as much data as possible, but a confidence level can be used to determine what we show, based on how correct we need our data to be. When multiple sources match exactly, it should be considered a higher confidence than one within a point or two. | **Changed.** The AI proposed three levels; I split "agree" into an exact and a close match. Per-source precision came from the AI's audit (#70).                                                     |
| 64–66             | A small embedded dashboard, rebuilt around three questions (is sync healthy, what just happened, where each price comes from), plus a README "How to verify" table. Its scenario buttons call the simulator's admin API, which allows requests from the dashboard's address.                                                                  | Visualization makes the behavior easier to see. The first version was information without meaning, so reviewers couldn't tell what to validate.                                                                                                                            | **Reworked.** I rejected the first dashboard as noise.                                                                                                                                               |
